Coplanar Concurrent Equilibrium

Understanding the equilibrium of forces acting in the same plane and passing through a common point.

1. Introduction

In engineering mechanics, equilibrium refers to a condition in which a body remains at rest or continues moving with constant velocity because the resultant external force acting on it is zero.

A coplanar concurrent force system consists of forces that satisfy two important conditions:

  • Coplanar: All forces act in the same plane, usually represented by the X-Y plane.
  • Concurrent: The lines of action of all forces pass through one common point.

When these forces balance one another, the particle remains in equilibrium.

2. Statement

For a coplanar concurrent force system to remain in equilibrium, the algebraic sum of all horizontal force components and the algebraic sum of all vertical force components must each be zero.

ΣFx = 0
ΣFy = 0

3. Conditions of Equilibrium

A. Horizontal Equilibrium

The total force acting along the horizontal X-axis must be zero.

ΣFx = 0

B. Vertical Equilibrium

The total force acting along the vertical Y-axis must be zero.

ΣFy = 0

Important Note

Since all forces pass through the same point, their moment arms about that common point are zero. Therefore, no separate moment equation is required for an ideal coplanar concurrent force system.

4. Mathematical Formulation

Consider a force F acting at an angle θ with the positive horizontal X-axis.

Its horizontal component is:

Fx = F cos θ

Its vertical component is:

Fy = F sin θ

For a system containing multiple forces:

Σ(F cos θ) = 0
Σ(F sin θ) = 0

Remember: Use positive or negative signs according to the chosen coordinate directions.

6. Derivation of Equilibrium Equations

Consider a particle subjected to several forces F₁, F₂, F₃, ... acting in one plane.

According to Newton's second law:

ΣF = ma

For equilibrium, acceleration is zero.

a = 0

Therefore:

ΣF = 0

Resolving this vector equation along the X-axis:

ΣFx = 0

Resolving along the Y-axis:

ΣFy = 0

Hence, the two independent scalar equations of equilibrium are obtained.

7. Solved Numerical Example

Problem

Two perpendicular forces of 100 N and 80 N act on a particle. Determine the magnitude and direction of the force required to maintain equilibrium.

Given Data

  • Horizontal force = 100 N
  • Vertical force = 80 N

Step 1: Resolve the forces

Horizontal direction:

ΣFx = 100 + F3x = 0
F3x = -100 N

Vertical direction:

ΣFy = 80 + F3y = 0
F3y = -80 N

Step 2: Calculate the magnitude

F3 = √(F3x² + F3y²)
F3 = √(100² + 80²)
F3 = 128.06 N

Step 3: Calculate the direction

θ = tan⁻¹(80 / 100)
θ = 38.66°

Final Answer

Balancing Force = 128.06 N

Its direction is 38.66° below the negative X-axis.

8. Engineering Applications

1. Structural Trusses

Forces at pin-connected truss joints are often analysed as concurrent force systems to determine internal member forces.

2. Guy Wires and Cables

Multiple cables connected to a common point can be analysed to determine the tension required for equilibrium.

3. Cranes and Rigging Systems

When several sling cables meet at a common hook, equilibrium equations help determine the forces carried by each cable.

4. Biomechanical Joints

Muscle forces, tendon forces and external loads acting around a joint can be studied using concurrent force systems.
