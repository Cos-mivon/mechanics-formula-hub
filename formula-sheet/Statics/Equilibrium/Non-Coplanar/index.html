Non-Coplanar Non-Concurrent Equilibrium

Three-dimensional force systems acting at different points on a rigid body.

1. Introduction

In engineering mechanics, forces do not always act in the same plane or pass through a common point. When forces act in different directions and at different points in three-dimensional space, the system is studied as a non-coplanar non-concurrent force system.

To maintain equilibrium, the body must have neither translational acceleration nor rotational acceleration.

2. Statement

A rigid body subjected to a non-coplanar non-concurrent force system is in equilibrium when the resultant force and the resultant moment about any reference point are both zero.

3. Conditions of Equilibrium

There are six independent scalar equilibrium conditions in three dimensions.

Force equilibrium:
ΣFx = 0
ΣFy = 0
ΣFz = 0
Moment equilibrium:
ΣMx = 0
ΣMy = 0
ΣMz = 0

4. Mathematical Formulation

For a force F acting at position r relative to the chosen origin, its moment is calculated using the vector cross product:

M = r × F

In component form:

Mx = yFz − zFy
My = zFx − xFz
Mz = xFy − yFx

The total moment is the vector sum of the moments produced by all forces.

6. Derivation of Equilibrium Equations

Step 1: Translational equilibrium

For no translational acceleration, Newton's second law gives:

ΣF = ma = 0

Resolving this vector equation along three axes:

ΣFx = 0,   ΣFy = 0,   ΣFz = 0

Step 2: Rotational equilibrium

For no angular acceleration, the resultant moment must be zero:

ΣM = 0

Resolving along the three coordinate axes:

ΣMx = 0,   ΣMy = 0,   ΣMz = 0

7. Solved Example — Force and Moment Resultants

Consider a rigid body subjected to several forces at different three-dimensional positions. For each force, calculate its moment about the origin using:

Mi = ri × Fi

Add all force components to obtain the resultant force, and add all moment components to obtain the resultant moment. The body is in equilibrium only when all six resultant components are zero.

8. Engineering Applications

  • Analysis of three-dimensional structures and space frames.
  • Forces acting on machine components.
  • Structural supports and complex frameworks.
  • Crane and lifting-system analysis.
  • Mechanical systems subjected to forces in different directions.
