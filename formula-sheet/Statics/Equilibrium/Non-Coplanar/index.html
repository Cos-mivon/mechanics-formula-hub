Non-Coplanar Non-Concurrent Equilibrium

Understanding three-dimensional forces acting at different points on a rigid body.

1. Introduction

In engineering structures and machines, forces may act in different planes and at different points on a body.

When the forces do not lie in one plane and their lines of action do not pass through a common point, the system is called a non-coplanar non-concurrent force system.

Such systems are analysed in three-dimensional space using force and moment equilibrium.

2. Statement

A rigid body subjected to a non-coplanar non-concurrent force system is in equilibrium when the resultant force and the resultant moment about any reference point are both zero.

3. Conditions of Equilibrium

For a rigid body to remain in complete equilibrium, six conditions must be satisfied.

Force Equilibrium
ΣFx = 0
ΣFy = 0
ΣFz = 0
Moment Equilibrium
ΣMx = 0
ΣMy = 0
ΣMz = 0

The first three conditions prevent translational acceleration. The remaining three prevent rotational acceleration.

4. Mathematical Formulation

The moment produced by a force depends on both its magnitude and the position at which it acts.

M = r × F

Where:

  • r = position vector from the reference point.
  • F = applied force vector.
  • M = moment of the force.

Resolving the moment into three components:

Mx = yFz − zFy
My = zFx − xFz
Mz = xFy − yFx

The total moment is obtained by adding the moments of all forces about the same reference point.

5. Derivation of Equilibrium Equations

Step 1 — Translational equilibrium

According to Newton's second law, the resultant force is equal to mass multiplied by acceleration.

ΣF = ma

For equilibrium, acceleration is zero. Therefore:

ΣFx = 0,   ΣFy = 0,   ΣFz = 0

Step 2 — Rotational equilibrium

For rotational equilibrium, the resultant moment about a reference point must be zero.

ΣM = 0

Resolving along the three axes:

ΣMx = 0,   ΣMy = 0,   ΣMz = 0

Thus, a three-dimensional rigid body has six independent scalar equilibrium equations.

6. Solved Example

A force F = (20i + 30j + 40k) N acts at a point whose position vector from the origin is:

r = (2i + 1j + 0.5k) m

Calculate the moment of the force about the origin.

Step 1 — Write the moment equation
M = r × F
Step 2 — Resolve the components
Mx = yFz − zFy
= (1 × 40) − (0.5 × 30)
= 25 Nm
My = zFx − xFz
= (0.5 × 20) − (2 × 40)
= −70 Nm
Mz = xFy − yFx
= (2 × 30) − (1 × 20)
= 40 Nm
Step 3 — Write the final answer
Mx = 25 Nm
My = −70 Nm
Mz = 40 Nm

Note: This example calculates the moment produced by one force. Complete equilibrium requires all six equilibrium conditions to be satisfied by the entire force system.

7. Engineering Applications

  • Three-dimensional structural frames.
  • Mechanical components and machine assemblies.
  • Crane and lifting systems.
  • Complex support and connection systems.
  • Engineering structures subjected to forces in multiple directions.
