Types of Loads

Understand different loading systems acting on structural members.

1. Introduction

A load is an external force or action applied to a structural member or mechanical body.

Depending on how the load is distributed over a member, it can be classified into different types.

In engineering mechanics, distributed loads are often replaced by an equivalent resultant force to simplify equilibrium calculations.

2. Point Load (Concentrated Load)

A point load acts at a particular point on a structural member. Its area of application is considered negligible compared with the member's dimensions.

Equivalent Resultant

R = P

The resultant acts at the same point and in the same direction as the applied point load.

Example: A concentrated weight placed on a beam.

3. Uniformly Distributed Load (UDL)

A UDL is a load distributed uniformly over a certain length of a member.

Its intensity remains constant throughout the loaded length.

Equivalent Resultant

W = w × L

Where:

  • w = Load intensity (kN/m)
  • L = Length over which the load acts (m)
  • W = Equivalent resultant force (kN)

Location of Resultant

x = L/2

The resultant acts at the centre of the uniformly loaded length.

Example: Uniformly distributed weight of a slab acting on a supporting beam.

4. Uniformly Varying Load (UVL)

A UVL is a distributed load whose intensity changes uniformly along the length of a member.

It commonly has a triangular load distribution.

Equivalent Resultant

W = ½ × w × L

The resultant equals the area of the triangular load distribution.

Location of Resultant

x = 2L/3 from zero-intensity end
x = L/3 from maximum-intensity end

The resultant acts through the centroid of the triangular load distribution.

Example: A linearly increasing load acting on a beam.

5. Trapezoidal Distributed Load

A trapezoidal load is a distributed load whose intensity varies linearly from one non-zero value to another non-zero value.

Equivalent Resultant

W = [(w₁ + w₂)/2] × L

Where w₁ and w₂ are the load intensities at the left and right ends respectively.

Location of Resultant

Measured from the left end, where the intensity is w₁:

x = L(w₁ + 2w₂) / [3(w₁ + w₂)]

The resultant acts through the centroid of the trapezoidal load distribution.

If w₁ = 0, the expression reduces to the triangular UVL case, with the resultant at 2L/3 from the left.

6. Summary of Equivalent Loads

  • Point Load: W = P
  • UDL: W = wL, acting at L/2
  • Triangular UVL: W = wL/2, acting at L/3 from maximum intensity
  • Trapezoidal Load: W = (w₁+w₂)L/2, with its centroid given above.

7. Engineering Applications

  • Analysis of beams and structural members.
  • Design of bridges and building structures.
  • Analysis of water pressure on retaining structures.
  • Study of distributed weight on supporting members.
  • Calculation of equivalent forces for equilibrium problems.
