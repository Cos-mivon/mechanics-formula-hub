Unit 1: Force System

Fundamentals, classification and laws of forces

01 · Fundamentals

1. Introduction to Force

A force is a push or pull acting on a body, or an action that tends to change the state of rest or uniform motion of a body.

Force is a vector quantity. Therefore, it requires both magnitude and direction for its complete representation.

SI Unit of Force = Newton (N)

Characteristics of Force

  1. Magnitude: The quantitative strength of a force. Example: 90 N.
  2. Direction: The orientation in which the force acts.
  3. Point of Application: The exact point at which the force acts on a body.
  4. Line of Action: An imaginary infinite straight line passing through the point of application in the direction of the force.

Illustration of a force acting on a body.

02 · Classification

2. Force System

When two or more forces act simultaneously on a body, they form a force system.

Classification Based on Spatial Orientation

A. Coplanar Force System

A force system in which all the force vectors lie in the same two-dimensional plane is called a coplanar force system.

  • Example: Forces acting on a ladder resting against a wall.
  • Example: Forces acting on a beam in a plane.

B. Non-Coplanar Force System

A force system in which the forces do not all lie in one common plane is called a non-coplanar or spatial force system.

  • Example: Forces acting on a three-dimensional structure.
  • Example: Forces acting on a body in different spatial directions.

Subcategories of Coplanar Force Systems

  1. Collinear Forces: Forces whose lines of action lie along the same straight line.
  2. Concurrent Force System: A system in which the lines of action of all forces pass through one common point.
  3. Parallel Force System: A system in which the lines of action of all forces are parallel to one another.

General Force System

As described in the supplied notes, a non-concurrent, non-coplanar force system is also referred to as a general force system.

Important distinction: Non-collinear means that forces do not share the same line of action. Non-coplanar means that forces do not all lie in the same plane. These terms are not interchangeable.
03 · Important Principle

3. Principle of Transmissibility of Force

Statement

The external effect of a force on a rigid body remains unchanged if the force is moved to any other point along its line of action, provided its magnitude and direction remain the same.

The force may be shifted along its line of action on a rigid body.

Explanation

A force acting on a rigid body can be transferred to another point on the same line of action without changing its external effect on the body.

Applications

  • Simplifying force systems in engineering mechanics.
  • Drawing and analysing free-body diagrams.
  • Structural design and mechanical assemblies.
  • Analysing forces in robotics and gear mechanisms.

Limitations

  • The principle applies to rigid-body external effects.
  • It does not mean that internal stresses or deformation remain unchanged when the force is shifted.
  • It should not be applied directly to deformable-body stress analysis.
04 · Law of Forces

4. Parallelogram Law of Forces

Statement

If two forces acting simultaneously on a particle are represented in magnitude and direction by two adjacent sides of a parallelogram drawn from a common point, their resultant is represented in magnitude and direction by the diagonal passing through that point.

Two forces P and Q act at a common point. Their diagonal represents the resultant R.

Formula for Resultant Magnitude

Let P and Q be two forces acting at an angle θ to each other. The magnitude of their resultant is:

R = √(P² + Q² + 2PQ cos θ)

Direction of Resultant

If α is the angle made by the resultant with force P:

tan α = (Q sin θ) / (P + Q cos θ)

Special Cases

  • When θ = 0°: R = P + Q
  • When θ = 90°: R = √(P² + Q²)
  • When θ = 180°: R = |P − Q|

Derivation

Resolve force Q into components along and perpendicular to P:

Rx = P + Q cos θ

Ry = Q sin θ

Using the Pythagorean theorem:

R = √(Rx² + Ry²)

R = √[(P + Q cos θ)² + (Q sin θ)²]

R = √(P² + Q² + 2PQ cos θ)

Solved Example

Given: P = 30 N, Q = 40 N, θ = 90°.

Step 1: Apply the formula.

R = √(P² + Q² + 2PQ cos θ)

R = √(30² + 40² + 2 × 30 × 40 × cos 90°)

R = √(900 + 1600)

Resultant, R = 50 N

Step 2: Find direction.

tan α = (40 × sin 90°) / (30 + 40 × cos 90°)

tan α = 40/30

α = 53.13°

Applications

  • Navigation and aviation for combining velocity vectors.
  • Robotics and computer graphics for combining vector movements.
  • Engineering analysis of two forces acting at a point.
05 · Law of Forces

5. Triangle Law of Forces

Statement

If two forces acting on a particle are represented in magnitude and direction by two sides of a triangle taken in order, their resultant is represented by the third side of the triangle taken in the opposite order.

Force P is followed by force Q. The closing side represents the resultant R.

Formula

If P and Q are the two forces and θ is the angle between them:

R = √(P² + Q² + 2PQ cos θ)

The direction can be found using:

tan α = (Q sin θ) / (P + Q cos θ)

Explanation

Draw the first force to scale. From the head of the first vector, draw the second force in its given direction. The vector joining the tail of the first force to the head of the second force gives the resultant.

The Triangle Law and Parallelogram Law are two graphical ways of representing the addition of two force vectors. They give the same resultant.

Solved Example

Given: P = 6 N, Q = 8 N, angle between them = 90°.

R = √(6² + 8² + 2 × 6 × 8 × cos 90°)

R = √(36 + 64)

Resultant, R = 10 N

tan α = 8/6

α = 53.13° measured from P towards Q.

Applications

  • Finding the resultant of two forces acting at a point.
  • Combining displacement and velocity vectors.
  • Graphical analysis of forces in engineering problems.
06 · Law of Forces

6. Polygon Law of Forces

Statement

If several forces acting simultaneously on a particle are represented in magnitude and direction by the sides of a polygon taken in order, their resultant is represented by the closing side of the polygon taken in the opposite order.

Multiple forces are drawn head-to-tail. The closing vector gives their resultant.

Formula Using Components

For several forces acting in a plane, resolve every force into horizontal and vertical components.

ΣFx = F1x + F2x + ... + Fnx

ΣFy = F1y + F2y + ... + Fny

The resultant magnitude is:

R = √[(ΣFx)² + (ΣFy)²]

The direction measured from the positive X-axis is:

θ = tan⁻¹[(ΣFy) / (ΣFx)]

The correct quadrant must be considered when determining the direction from the component signs.

Condition for Equilibrium

If the force polygon closes, the resultant is zero and the particle is in equilibrium.

ΣFx = 0

ΣFy = 0

Solved Example

Three forces act on a particle:

  • F₁ = 10 N along the positive X-axis.
  • F₂ = 10 N along the positive Y-axis.
  • F₃ = 10 N along the negative X-axis.

Step 1: Horizontal components

ΣFₓ = 10 + 0 − 10 = 0 N

Step 2: Vertical components

ΣFᵧ = 0 + 10 + 0 = 10 N

Step 3: Resultant

R = √(0² + 10²)

Resultant, R = 10 N upward.

Applications

  • Finding the resultant of three or more forces.
  • Analysing forces acting on structures and joints.
  • Solving engineering problems involving multiple forces.
  • Checking equilibrium of a particle.
07 · Revision

7. Quick Revision

Important Concepts

  • Force is a vector quantity.
  • A force system consists of two or more forces acting on a body.
  • Coplanar forces lie in the same plane.
  • Concurrent forces have lines of action passing through a common point.
  • The principle of transmissibility applies to rigid-body external effects.

Important Formulae

Parallelogram / Triangle Law:
R = √(P² + Q² + 2PQ cos θ)
Direction:
tan α = (Q sin θ) / (P + Q cos θ)
Polygon Law:
R = √[(ΣFₓ)² + (ΣFᵧ)²]
Equilibrium of a particle:
ΣFₓ = 0,   ΣFᵧ = 0
